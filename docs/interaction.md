# Interactions, mechanisms and physical passages

Source requirements are DOCX §3 (simple rune/lever mechanisms), §4 (forbidden library books and hidden doors), §6–8 (mandatory fights, optional branch and final exit), with the explicit topology in `design-decisions.md` and `level-contract.json`. This implementation uses the scene's existing `LevelProgressionController` rather than a second progression state.

`PlayerInteraction` subscribes to `PlayerMovement.InteractRequested` during its enabled lifecycle. Each E press selects exactly one nearest usable collider in a 2.6 metre sphere and checks its line of sight against solid geometry. It does not interact through a wall. The range, three rune symbols and exact sequence are implementation choices. The scene displays the ordered rune hint next to the mechanism; an incorrect rune clears only the partial sequence and repeats the hint in the HUD, allowing unlimited retries without consumed items or softlocks.

`RunePuzzle` accepts the displayed sequence and reports `MainPuzzleSolved`; its deterministic `RuneSequence` source has headless C# regression tests. Rune clicks carry the SessionId captured by the interaction press. An old click cannot solve a mechanism after reset. Wrong input, duplicate completion and objective attempts from the wrong logical room do not open a gate.

The forbidden book in the library releases its concealed passage and reports `LibraryOpened`. The catacomb lever reports only `SecretLeverPulled`, opening the optional bonus branch and its concealed return to the throne room. The bonus relic reports `BonusDiscovered`; neither objective is required to enter the final arena or finish the level. `LevelInteractable` deliberately accepts only these three interaction objectives: kills and arbitrary progression flags cannot be granted by an E interaction.

The basic physical route is `Courtyard → FirstEncounter → Puzzle → FirstEncounter → ThroneRoom → Library → Catacombs → FinalArena → Exit`. The return from the western puzzle to the lower junction preserves the wall between puzzle and throne shown in the DOCX map. The three distinct encounters preserve the first threat, throne-room miniboss and final fight in the flow chart. Placing the mandatory library on the throne-to-catacombs connector is the explicit decision recorded in [design-decisions.md](design-decisions.md), because the prose specifies the library while the map does not label it separately. The exit requires all five mandatory objectives, including the final defeat; the map's “fight/scene” label does not add a required cutscene.

The optional upper-left branch is `Catacombs ↔ BonusRoom`, opened by the library objective plus the catacomb lever. The same lever opens `ThroneRoom ↔ BonusRoom`, also requiring the miniboss and library objectives. That concealed connection descends from the throne through a separate lower tunnel and ascends beside the bonus room, with a physical entrance seal and a separate doorway at the bonus end. It implements the flow chart's throne-room secret connection without merging the differently placed map areas or cutting through the main-route walls. Returning through it leads back through the library and catacombs before the final arena. A player can instead return from the bonus room by the upper branch, or omit both optional objectives and finish normally. The runtime has nine logical connections; the shortcut's second physical seal accounts for the tenth gate collider.

`PassageGate` subscribes to committed progression changes and mirrors `IsPassageOpen` onto its solid collider and closed-door renderers. It also reads a fresh snapshot when enabled, so a reset performed while the gate component is disabled is reflected when it returns. Builders must make the barrier fill the corridor width and extend above the player's jump; the level builder uses a 7 metre barrier and a 10 metre trigger height, reaching the vault and exceeding the configured jump.

`RoomTransition` uses a tall doorway trigger and a crossing plane pointing from its source safe anchor to its destination safe anchor, projected onto the horizontal plane. The player must cross that plane before logical `TryEnter` occurs. It captures the session at trigger entry and never replaces that token inside a stale trigger callback. Rejected crossing returns the character to the source/destination safe anchor corresponding to the logical room, clearing accumulated motion. The anchors must lie on their side of the doorway, outside the trigger volume. The lower puzzle route therefore returns to the junction, and no wall-cutting puzzle-to-throne connection is added. Gates remain open until reset.

Configure hooks are provided for scene assembly: `RunePuzzle.Configure`, `LevelInteractable.ConfigureRune/ConfigureObjective/ConfigureHint`, `PlayerInteraction.Configure`, `PassageGate.Configure` and `RoomTransition.Configure`. Scene components own the physical reset; progression observers only update local mechanism/presentation state and do not synchronously change progression from a `Changed` listener.

`LevelHud` renders a scene-owned UGUI `ScreenSpaceCamera` Canvas through the actual follow camera. Its 1280 × 720 reference layout displays health, current objective, controls, elapsed time, interaction prompt/feedback, enemy health and attack cues, and the defeat/victory message with optional relic status. A real restart `Button` and R input are available only at a terminal state. The Canvas and one scene-owned `EventSystem`/`InputSystemUIInputModule` are configured while inactive; UI actions use an owned copy of the existing asset and are disabled/released with their lifecycle. Button listeners capture the terminal SessionId. R callbacks capture their session before queueing, and the scene rejects an old token, focus loss, pause or a nonterminal state before resetting. The UI updates presentation without issuing progression commands from `Changed` observers. Font, colours and panel layout are implementation choices rather than DOCX requirements.

Verification commands:

```sh
dotnet test tests/Puzzles/Puzzles.Tests.csproj --configuration Release
python3 tools/validate_level_contract.py

```

`LevelInteractionTests` in the Unity PlayMode assembly cover runtime rune retries/session reset, physical barrier enable/reset, single visible target selection and rejected/accepted trigger callbacks. The callback tests exercise the real Unity components but do not by themselves prove the scene's trigger geometry or a full manual playthrough. Real Unity test execution, final player traversal and the 2–3 minute timing target must be reported separately from the headless mechanism tests.

`ForsakenLevelTests` loads the actual `ForsakenCastle` scene. Its basic route drives `CharacterController.Move` at the configured scene speed per real fixed frame, lets the production player/enemy `Update` methods execute attack windows, selects mechanisms through `PlayerInteraction` range/line-of-sight checks, and crosses physical doorway triggers. It asserts terminal-only restart, all three defeats, library progression, completion without optional flags, normal player vulnerability, the 120–180 second automated route window, and whole-scene reset. The victory case invokes the real Button's `onClick` and production listener; an R callback queued before that reset must not reset the new session a second time. The defeat case delivers actual R input through the Input System, checks rejection while unfocused or paused and after a queued press loses focus, then verifies restart with restored test focus. Neither path uses a direct `TryRestart` call to perform the terminal reset.

Separate regressions traverse the optional upper branch and lower return ramps, wait for enemy-inflicted defeat, and attempt an airborne crossing of a closed gate. Those optional/failure regressions use faster fixture stepping and cannot establish a gameplay duration. Evidence JSON records the distinction, measured distance/time, health and waypoints. The delivery tests require a graphics device and save 1280 × 720 PNGs through URP 17's supported `SingleCameraRequest` rendered into a `RenderTexture` and read with `ReadPixels`; no batch-mode end-of-frame wait is used. These captures include the world camera and the real camera-space Canvas HUD. A uniform image fails the visual-evidence check, and living-player captures additionally check the actual health graphic's pixels at its viewport position. A separate defeat capture records the terminal UI. Safe camera poses are asserted at route waypoints. Synthetic input uses the pinned package's official `InputTestFixture` to isolate and restore device/runtime state, plus explicit Manual updates and component focus/pause callbacks; production focus and input guards remain active. These are source-defined checks, not a claim that the current final engine run passed. Actual results belong in the verification report. Automated timing, Button-listener execution and captured UI pixels are distinct from a human playtest, a physical mouse-click playtest and a measured full-route player-build performance benchmark.
