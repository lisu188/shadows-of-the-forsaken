# Interactions, mechanisms and physical passages

Source requirements are DOCX §3 (simple rune/lever mechanisms), §4 (forbidden library books and hidden doors), §6–8 (mandatory fights, optional branch and final exit), with the explicit topology in `design-decisions.md` and `level-contract.json`. This implementation uses the scene's existing `LevelProgressionController` rather than a second progression state.

`PlayerInteraction` subscribes to `PlayerMovement.InteractRequested` during its enabled lifecycle. Each E press selects exactly one nearest usable collider in a 2.6 metre sphere and checks its line of sight against solid geometry. It does not interact through a wall. The range, three rune symbols and exact sequence are implementation choices. The scene displays the ordered rune hint next to the mechanism; an incorrect rune clears only the partial sequence and repeats the hint in the HUD, allowing unlimited retries without consumed items or softlocks.

`RunePuzzle` accepts the displayed sequence and reports `MainPuzzleSolved`; its deterministic `RuneSequence` source has headless C# regression tests. Rune clicks carry the SessionId captured by the interaction press. An old click cannot solve a mechanism after reset. Wrong input, duplicate completion and objective attempts from the wrong logical room do not open a gate.

The forbidden book in the library releases its concealed passage and reports `LibraryOpened`. The catacomb lever reports only `SecretLeverPulled`, opening the optional bonus branch and its concealed return to the throne room. The bonus relic reports `BonusDiscovered`; neither objective is required to enter the final arena or finish the level. `LevelInteractable` deliberately accepts only these three interaction objectives: kills and arbitrary progression flags cannot be granted by an E interaction.

`PassageGate` subscribes to committed progression changes and mirrors `IsPassageOpen` onto its solid collider and closed-door renderers. It also reads a fresh snapshot when enabled, so a reset performed while the gate component is disabled is reflected when it returns. Builders must make the barrier fill the corridor width and extend above the player's jump; the level builder uses a 7 metre barrier and a 10 metre trigger height, reaching the vault and exceeding the configured jump.

`RoomTransition` uses a tall doorway trigger and a crossing plane pointing from its source safe anchor to its destination safe anchor, projected onto the horizontal plane. The player must cross that plane before logical `TryEnter` occurs. It captures the session at trigger entry and never replaces that token inside a stale trigger callback. Rejected crossing returns the character to the source/destination safe anchor corresponding to the logical room, clearing accumulated motion. The anchors must lie on their side of the doorway, outside the trigger volume. The lower puzzle route therefore returns to the junction, and no wall-cutting puzzle-to-throne connection is added. Gates remain open until reset.

Configure hooks are provided for scene assembly: `RunePuzzle.Configure`, `LevelInteractable.ConfigureRune/ConfigureObjective/ConfigureHint`, `PlayerInteraction.Configure`, `PassageGate.Configure` and `RoomTransition.Configure`. Scene components own the physical reset; progression observers only update local mechanism/presentation state and do not synchronously change progression from a `Changed` listener.

Verification commands:

```sh
dotnet test tests/Puzzles/Puzzles.Tests.csproj --configuration Release
python3 tools/validate_level_contract.py
python3 tools/unity_validation.py project
```

`LevelInteractionTests` in the Unity PlayMode assembly cover runtime rune retries/session reset, physical barrier enable/reset, single visible target selection and rejected/accepted trigger callbacks. The callback tests exercise the real Unity components but do not by themselves prove the scene's trigger geometry or a full manual playthrough. Real Unity test execution, final player traversal and the 2–3 minute timing target must be reported separately from the headless mechanism tests.

`ForsakenLevelTests` loads the actual `ForsakenCastle` scene. Its basic route drives `CharacterController.Move` at the configured scene speed per real fixed frame, lets the production player/enemy `Update` methods execute attack windows, selects mechanisms through `PlayerInteraction` range/line-of-sight checks, and crosses physical doorway triggers. It asserts terminal-only restart, all three defeats, library progression, completion without optional flags, normal player vulnerability, the 120–180 second automated route window, and whole-scene reset. Separate regressions traverse the optional upper branch and lower return ramps, wait for enemy-inflicted defeat, and attempt an airborne crossing of a closed gate. Those optional/failure regressions use faster fixture stepping and cannot establish a gameplay duration. Evidence JSON records the distinction, measured distance/time, health and waypoints. The delivery tests require a graphics device and save 1280 × 720 PNGs through URP 17's supported `SingleCameraRequest` rendered into a `RenderTexture` and read with `ReadPixels`; no batch-mode end-of-frame wait is used. These captures show the world camera and exclude the immediate-mode HUD. A uniform captured image fails the visual-evidence check. Safe camera poses are asserted at route waypoints. Automated timing and captures are distinct from a human playtest and a measured player-build performance benchmark. Synthetic input uses the pinned package's official `InputTestFixture` to isolate and restore device/runtime state, plus explicit Manual updates; production focus and input guards are retained.
